SYNTHETIC FINANCIAL RESEARCH NOTE
Title: Macroeconomic Conditions and Cross-Asset Risk Factors
Date: 2026-06-30
Source: Northstar Research, fictional training corpus

Growth backdrop
The fictional US economy expanded at a 1.8 percent annualised rate in the first quarter of 2026.
Consumer spending slowed from the prior year but remained positive, supported by real wage growth and
low unemployment. Business investment was split: spending on AI data centres and automation remained
strong, while office equipment and non-residential construction outside technology were subdued.

Inflation and bonds
Core services inflation remained near 3 percent, with shelter and insurance costs offsetting lower
goods prices. A slower return to the 2 percent target could keep real yields and the term premium
elevated. Government borrowing remained high, increasing supply of long-dated bonds. A higher term
premium can increase the discount rate used by equity investors even without a central-bank rate hike.

Downside scenario
The downside scenario combines sticky inflation, a ten-year yield above 4.7 percent, and a softer
consumer. Technology equities then face lower earnings confidence and valuation compression. AI
infrastructure demand would be more resilient than discretionary software spending, but suppliers
could be affected if cloud providers cut capital-expenditure plans.
